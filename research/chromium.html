Problem Statement

Groundwater is the main source of drinking and irrigation water in Bangladesh, yet it faces rising stress from overuse and pollution. In Khulna’s urban fringe, the Rajbandh open dumpsite produces leachate that can carry Cr into the aquifer, but its long-term impact is not quantified due to limited monitoring and the absence of a site-specific simulation model. The study area is shifting from agriculture to housing, and new wells may be installed near the dumpsite. Without a calibrated flow and transport model, the risk of chronic exposure through groundwater remains uncertain, and planning for safe abstraction or control measures is largely guesswork. The problem is sharpened by scarce hydrological and water-quality data, which hampers reliable prediction and decision-making. • The dumpsite lacks engineered containment and monitoring, allowing leachate to infiltrate and migrate with groundwater. • Heavy metals detected in landfill leachate elsewhere are toxic and persistent, posing serious health risks even at low levels. • No calibrated contamination transport model exists for Rajbandh to simulate long-term plume migration, breakthrough timing. • Decision-makers lack evidence to set well setback distances, design monitoring networks, or evaluate management options under limited data. Therefore, the core problem is the absence of a validated, long-term groundwater flow and solute transport model for a data-scarce urban fringe dumpsite that can quantify heavy metal plume evolution, estimate exposure at existing and future wells, and inform risk-based management in Khulna.

Objective

  • To assess and predict long-term migration of Cr in groundwater of Rajbandh landfill.
  • To assess groundwater flow direction and predict water level depletion in the dry season of Jalma union, Khulna.
  • To assess hydraulic conductivity as a key parameter in an advection-dominated transport system.
  • To establish a relation between elapsed time and the affected area using projection analysis.

Methodology

To simulate the transportation of heavy metals, it is essential to first develop a groundwater simulation model. Consequently, the entire study is divided into two sections: the development of a groundwater model and the subsequent simulation of heavy metal transportation. Model Muse 5.3.1.0 was employed as the graphical user interface (GUI) for the groundwater model, while MODFLOW 2005 and MT3DMS were employed for groundwater modelling and heavy metal transport, respectively. Arc GIS 10.8 is used for data preparation and contamination mapping.

The simulation results demonstrate a progressive expansion of chromium-affected areas (> 0.05 mg/L) over the 33-year modeling period, with varying rates of change across different temporal intervals. The contamination plume area exhibited consistent growth throughout the simulation, expanding from an initial area of 0.15 km² in year 1 to 0.66 km² by year 33, representing an overall increase of 0.51 km² or 340% total expansion over the entire simulation period.

Key Findings

  • Cr migration tends to the direction of NW which is aligning with the direction of groundwater flow. So, a predominantly advection-controlled migration pattern.
  • The simulation results show characteristic longitudinal spreading patterns where the plume progressively elongates downgradient from the landfill source, demonstrating the influence of longitudinal dispersivity on contaminant distribution.
  • The breakthrough time analysis demonstrates that natural clay layers offer a critical 20-year window of effective protection before chromium concentrations in the underlying sand aquifer exceed WHO drinking water limits, with the clay’s low hydraulic conductivity and sorption capacity significantly retarding contaminant migration.
  • The comparative analysis between clay and sand layers reveals the fundamental role of hydraulic conductivity as the controlling parameter in contaminant migration patterns.
